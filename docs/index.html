Why We Built This

A story that should
never happen again

When I was little, my pregnant aunt got sick. But nobody noticed quickly enough. She did not feel well, the warning signs were there, but without anyone watching, without any way to know, the danger crept in silently.

By the time she almost fainted, it was too late to prevent what had already happened inside the womb. The baby had suffered an injury. That injury did not heal before birth.

"My cousin was born with a hole in her back (spina bifida). A condition that could have been caught. Could have been treated. Could have been prevented if someone had been watching earlier."

After weeks in hospital and surgery, the doctors confirmed what the family feared. My cousin will not have full control of her bladder she does not know when she needs the bathroom. She was also born with clubfoot. She is still treating this today.

This is not a rare story. Across Nigeria, across Africa, across the world — this story repeats every single day. Mothers who do not know they are in danger. Babies who pay the price. Families who live with consequences that were never inevitable.

What SurviveMum changes
If a phone had been watching my aunt reading her vital signs, tracking her blood pressure trends, alerting a Traditional Birth Attendant before she almost fainted my cousin might have been born healthy. That phone exists now. That AI exists now. SurviveMum is built for every family that should never have to go through what mine did.

Gemma 4 Capabilities

Six capabilities.
All load-bearing.

01 — Thinking Traces
Clinical Reasoning Visible

Every alert shows Gemma's complete reasoning — which guideline it applied, which threshold was crossed. The TBA sees exactly why the alert fired.

02 — Native Function Calling
Auto-Generated Referrals

When an alert fires, Gemma writes a complete medical referral letter in 3 seconds — in Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo. It reaches the hospital before the mother arrives.

03 — LiteRT Edge Deploy
$80 Android Phone

Gemma 4 runs entirely on-device using LiteRT. No internet required. No cloud. Works in communities hours from the nearest hospital.

04 — On-Device RAG
WHO Guidelines On-Device

Before every assessment, Gemma retrieves the most relevant WHO guideline from a local FAISS index. Every recommendation is grounded in verifiable clinical evidence.

05 — Unsloth Fine-Tuning
Nigerian Clinical Intelligence

Fine-tuned on Nigeria MDSR maternal death audit patterns. Training loss reduced from 8.2 to 2.4 across 650 steps over 50 clinical records.

06 — Audio Multimodal
Cry Classification

High-pitched short bursts → possible meningitis. Weak breathy cry → respiratory distress. Four clinical categories classified in real time from the phone microphone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need
to know

Does SurviveMum work without internet?
+
Yes — 100% offline. The Gemma 4 AI model, WHO clinical guidelines, cry classification rules, and all patient data are stored on the device. The app has never made a network call for any clinical feature. Internet is only used once during initial setup to download the Gemma model file (1.3GB). After that, the app works in the most remote communities with no signal.
Is my patient data safe and private?
+
Completely. All patient records, vital signs, alerts, and health history are stored in an encrypted local database on the device. Nothing is ever sent to a server, cloud, or any third party. If the phone is lost, patient data cannot be accessed without the PIN. We never see your data — it never leaves the device.
How does the camera measure vital signs?
+
The technique is called remote photoplethysmography (rPPG). When the heart beats, it pumps blood through the skin, causing very subtle colour changes that are invisible to the eye but detectable by a camera. SurviveMum uses the CHROM algorithm — which was specifically chosen because it performs well on darker skin tones, unlike standard RGB methods. The camera watches the mother's face and extracts heart rate and breathing rate from these colour changes over a 5-second window.
How does the cry classification work?
+
The phone microphone records the baby's cry and extracts two key features — pitch frequency (how high the sound is in Hz) and burst duration (how long each cry lasts). High-pitched cries above 400Hz in short bursts under 0.5 seconds are associated with neurological distress and possible meningitis. Weak, breathy cries below 250Hz with long duration indicate respiratory distress. The app classifies into four categories: DISTRESS, RESPIRATORY DISTRESS, PAIN, and NORMAL — in real time.
What is a Traditional Birth Attendant (TBA)?
+
Traditional Birth Attendants are community health workers — often women — who assist with births and antenatal care in rural communities across Nigeria and Africa. They are often the only healthcare presence in communities hours from the nearest hospital. SurviveMum is designed to work in their hands, with a simple interface that requires no medical training, in Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo.
Where do the clinical guidelines come from?
+
All clinical recommendations are grounded in WHO guidelines — WHO ANC Guidelines 2022, WHO Essential Newborn Care 2024, WHO PPH Management Guidelines, and WHO Maternal Sepsis recommendations. Before every Gemma assessment, the app searches a local database of 14 clinical documents and retrieves the most relevant guideline. Every alert cites its source. No recommendation is generated without being grounded in a specific, verifiable WHO document.
Is SurviveMum a replacement for a doctor?
+
No. SurviveMum is a monitoring and early warning tool — not a diagnostic device. It is designed to help Traditional Birth Attendants and community health workers recognise danger signs earlier and refer patients to the appropriate facility faster. Every critical alert instructs the TBA to refer the patient immediately. The app closes the first delay — failure to recognise danger — it does not replace clinical care at the facility.
How much does SurviveMum cost?
+
Free. Completely free. No subscription, no in-app purchases, no premium tier. The app is open source under the Apache 2.0 licence. The only cost is the Android phone to run it on — which can be as inexpensive as $80. This was a deliberate design decision — the communities that need this most cannot afford subscription software.
